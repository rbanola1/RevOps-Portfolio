# Channel economics

All figures are illustrative assumptions on a fictional company.

## Contribution by route (share of list price)
| Route | Price kept after discount or fee | Cost to serve | Product cost | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Direct (web and reps) | 100% | 22% | 38% | 40% |
| Distributor | 70% | 6% | 38% | 26% |
| Marketplace | 92% | 4% | 38% | 50% |

**Reading it:** distributors give up the most margin per unit but unlock territories we cannot serve directly (cold chain, customs, local language). Marketplaces keep most of the margin and add reach into procurement-driven accounts, but listing and data work is real effort. Direct stays the largest route.

## Path to the channel target
| Quarter | Channel share of revenue |
|---|---|
| Q1 | 2% |
| Q2 | 5% |
| Q3 | 9% |
| Q4 | 13% |
| Q1 next | 17% |
| Q2 next | 21% |

Assumptions: total revenue grows about 5% per quarter; channel revenue splits 60% distributors and 40% marketplaces.

## What to watch
- Channel share of revenue and of new customers
- Contribution margin by route, not just revenue
- Time from first outreach to signed agreement, by tier
- Listing effort per platform against revenue earned
- Price integrity: no platform listing below the agreed price
