# Method

## Formulas
- **Wins needed** = revenue target / average order value.
- **Channel revenue** = target x channel share.
- **Channel wins** = channel revenue / channel order value.
- **Deals** = wins / deal-to-win rate.
- **SQLs** = deals / SQL-to-deal rate.
- **MQLs** = SQLs / MQL-to-SQL rate (only where an MQL stage exists for the channel).
- **Cost per win** = channel cost / wins. **ROI** = channel revenue / channel cost.

## Capacity validation (P x T)
| Term | Meaning | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| P (portfolio multiplier) | Market coverage: 0.2 per product line, up to 1.0 with five | 0.8 |
| T (trust factor) | Brand maturity and technical validation versus the strongest competitor (1.0) | 0.4 |
| Capacity | P x T: the highest close rate the team can credibly assume | 32% |

A channel passes if its required close rate is at or below capacity. Channels above it need a justification (for example a small, simple product with a different buyer) or a lower share of the plan.

## Benchmark
Past data may not give a clear close rate (different products, partial years), so use a benchmark near the observed average (about 27%) for planning and keep a margin below capacity.

## Repeat purchasers
- Assume a fixed number of constant repeat accounts (for example distributors).
- Assume a share (20%) of the remaining customers reorder.
- Minimum repeat customers = 20% x (wins - constant accounts) + constant accounts.
- Unique new customers needed = wins - repeat customers.

## Assumptions to state in every plan
Average order value, win rate per channel, one deal per unique customer unless stated, low-value channels (small products) modelled at their own order size, distributor revenue in the second half because of ramp time.

## Limits
Small samples give shaky conversion rates; marketing-qualified volumes are unreliable; the capacity formula is a judgement, not a law. Re-estimate each quarter as data accumulates.
