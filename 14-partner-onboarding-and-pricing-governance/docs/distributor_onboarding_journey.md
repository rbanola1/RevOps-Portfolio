# Distributor onboarding: the eight-stage journey

From identification to launch, a step-by-step framework for building channel sales. Each stage has an owner, must-ask questions and required documents.

| # | Stage | Primary owner | Target time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify and qualify | Commercial operations | Ongoing |
| 2 | Outreach and intro call | Commercial operations | 1 to 2 weeks |
| 3 | Compliance and logistics | Commercial operations with legal | 2 to 12 weeks, varies by country |
| 4 | NDA | Commercial operations with legal | 1 to 3 weeks |
| 5 | Pricing and product setup | Commercial operations with product management and marketing | 1 to 2 weeks |
| 6 | Distribution agreement | Commercial operations with legal | 3 to 8 weeks |
| 7 | Catalog and portal listing | Marketing with commercial operations | 2 to 4 weeks |
| 8 | Launch and ongoing management | Commercial operations | Continuous |

## Stage 1: identify potential partners
**Scoring criteria:** geography (gaps in current footprint), sales capability (researcher network, CRM maturity, team size), technical capability (cold chain: liquid nitrogen or dry ice, storage, handling), financial health (revenue scale, payment reliability, fulfilment record), synergy (complementary lines, direct or indirect competitor conflicts).
**Sourcing:** inbound web form, competitor distributor pages, conference introductions, LinkedIn outreach to distributor business-development leads, referrals from partners and advisors.
**Tracking:** a distribution strategy notebook for active conversations, and a channel partner list for every historical reach-out, including those not yet in the CRM.

### Must-ask discovery questions
| Topic | Questions |
|---|---|
| Coverage and reach | Which geography do you cover; exclusive by country? How many suppliers and SKUs? Who is on the team and what is their focus? |
| Existing suppliers | Do you carry competing cell lines? Who are your current suppliers? Note who else they represent: it can validate the category, signal conflict or complement the line. Discovery only: do not volunteer our existing distributor or marketplace partners |
| Cold chain and logistics | Liquid nitrogen, dry ice or both; disposable or reusable shipper? Some partners cannot handle liquid nitrogen imports, so confirm before the NDA. Any import limits on animal-derived components? |
| Commercial model | Exclusivity by product or region? Margin and discount structure? How do customers order: your website, direct PO or both? Any finder's fee? |
| Marketing | How do you promote new supplier lines? Will you list us in your online catalog? Do you attend conferences where co-promotion makes sense? |

## Stage 2: outreach and qualifying call
- **Methods:** cold email to the business-development or supplier-development contact, or the company's general address; LinkedIn connection with a short intro; follow up within 48 hours of a conference conversation. Lead with the platform, the absence of genetic modification or viral vectors, and the validated product pipeline.
- **On the intro call:** confirm decision-makers in the room (marketing, general manager, finance); present a two or three slide deck (who we are, pipeline, market sizing); probe exclusivity expectations early; clarify whether they earn on the distributor discount (the standard case) or need a per-sale commission (typically only marketplaces); ask who handles custom project inquiries; set the expectation of the path: compliance, NDA, pricing, agreement, listing.

## Stage 3: regulatory, compliance and logistics
- **Import and export permits:** some countries require permits to import, store and distribute biological materials; budget two to three months. Check before the NDA and agreement: if compliance does not clear, there is no point spending legal time.
- **Cold chain confirmation:** validate liquid nitrogen versus dry ice, and disposable versus reusable shipper, and re-confirm after stage 1. Serum-free media and cryo solutions simplify many customs reviews.
- **Storage and drop-ship:** confirm whether the partner stores stock in its own freezers or we drop-ship each order, and which product types it prefers not to stock.

## Stage 4: NDA
Send the standard mutual NDA. If the partner asks to use its own, route it to counsel before signature. File the executed copy in the distributor documents folder and the legal intake record. Some partners, for example in Europe, take two to four weeks for internal legal review.

## Stage 5: pricing and product information
Commercial operations: create a partner folder with the partner's price sheet; apply the **standard discount off list** from the current pricing sheet template; provide the product and price list in Excel; share data sheets, application notes and website links; confirm the PO email and remit-to address with the partner's finance contact; state each product's launch status and list only what is available, flagging upcoming launches with target dates; set the international shipping policy.

## Stage 6: distribution agreement and negotiation
| Term | Standard position |
|---|---|
| Exclusivity | By product, region or none; most partners do not require it, so confirm case by case |
| Discount or transfer price | Standard offer; any deviation needs executive approval |
| Territory | Named countries or regions |
| Minimum order quantity and minimum order value | Set both explicitly; they vary case by case |
| Payment terms | Net 30 or 60; remit-to details |
| International shipping | Absorb part of shipping in year one when the minimum order value is met, or let orders accumulate until volume justifies removing the fee |
| Template | Ours or theirs; faster when the partner's simple template is acceptable |
Counsel owns redlines; commercial operations shares drafts. Custom project or fee-for-service clauses may need a separate addendum.

## Stage 7: catalog listing and portal setup
Partner marketing sets up our listing: confirm the contact, provide images, descriptions and application notes per SKU, confirm the region toggle, and ask for a notice when live so we can check the listing. Ask whether we will get a general sales rep or a specialist; a dedicated specialist meaningfully raises the close rate. Share onboarding paperwork (tax forms, remit details) through finance; never paste bank or tax identifiers into commercial files.

## Stage 8: launch and promotion
- Joint announcement through the partner's channels and ours.
- Quarterly check-in on order volume, top SKUs and blockers.
- **CRM tagging:** set Company Type to "Distributor" once added; on a closed deal set Deal Source to "Distributor/Channel Sales". This is how distributor revenue is tracked separately for return on the channel.
- Re-engage dormant partners with each new launch.

## B2B marketplaces: a faster, separate track
1. **Initiate contact** through LinkedIn, the platform's supplier registration page or its supplier mailbox.
2. **Confirm commercial terms:** payment terms, commission or discount structure, and whether the standard discount is accepted.
3. **Internal alignment before registering:** product management confirms pricing and product origin; marketing owns keywords and page look; the executive approves any commission or discount deviation.
4. **Submit registration:** new supplier form, tax form, pricing template, user instructions, storage conditions and safety data sheets.
Build a buyer-side view of the marketplace first to see how competitors are listed and priced.

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Marketing manager | Keywords, page look, visual presentation |
| Commercial operations | Delegation, registration tracking, ongoing marketplace conversations |
| Product management | Pricing accuracy and product origin sheet |
| Executive | Approves standard discount and any deviation |

## Ongoing account maintenance
- **Monthly touchpoint** with each active distributor for troubleshooting and relationship building.
- **Pricing:** revise the price sheet when needed and redistribute promptly.
- **Training:** train the distributor's team so they sell, not just list.
- **Portal audit:** audit the distributor's listing for accuracy, completeness, search visibility; jointly with marketing.
- **KPIs and contract review:** set KPIs per distributor and update or end the contract if targets are missed. For exclusive partners this is non-negotiable: a partner must not sit on a territory while blocking others.
- **Marketing cadence:** send new data, launches and case studies regularly.

## Gaps identified
A standard distribution agreement template and a distributor pitch deck were the two open process gaps.
