# Lane criteria

> Used together with the warm-lead model: inside sales qualifies and nurtures inbound leads and hands warm leads to the rep who owns the account.

| Criterion | Inside sales | Outside sales | Business development | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Geography | Remote and phone, inbound | Field visits, domestic and international | Travels for partnerships | Volume handled remotely, strategic presence in the field |
| Sales cycle | Under 3 months | 3 months or more | Long, relationship-led | Quick inbound decisions versus strategic accounts |
| Source | Web forms, inbound calls, general email | Trade shows, events, referrals | Inquiries for specialised services | Inside is built for high-volume inbound, outside for face-to-face |
| Product category | Standard research products and technical support | Strategic research products | Therapeutics, custom and collaboration work | Product expertise match |
| Customer type | Academic, small biotech, service labs | Mid-size and large pharma | Large pharma and collaboration partners | Academics need quick response, large accounts need a strategic approach |
| Deal size | Up to about 20 kits | 20+ kits or high-value key accounts | Any size, due to complexity | Larger deals need relationship building |
| Response time | Within 24 hours | 24 hours or more, thoughtful follow-up | Within 72 hours | Urgency should match the buyer |

## Hand-offs
- Inside sales can nurture a contact or deal for a set period, then hand it to outside sales when it meets outside criteria.
- Conference and event leads default to the outside sales manager, who reassigns them.
- When criteria conflict, the account owner decides; if there is no owner, the lead goes to the lane that matches the product category.
